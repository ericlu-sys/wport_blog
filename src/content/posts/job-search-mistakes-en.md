Many overseas students take a long detour when they first start looking for work in Taiwan. It is rarely a question of ability. It is usually unfamiliarity with how the Taiwanese job market actually works. Here are the five most common mistakes, so you can avoid them early.

---

## Mistake 1: applying only to foreign companies or English-language roles

Most job openings in Taiwan are still Chinese-first. Many overseas students feel their Chinese is not good enough, so they apply only to English-language roles, which drastically shrinks their options.

**What to do instead:** work out exactly what level your Chinese communication is at, and state it clearly when you apply. Plenty of Taiwanese companies are willing to let a foreign colleague run meetings in English and write emails in Chinese. It is less rigid than you might think.

---

## Mistake 2: reusing your overseas resume format as-is

A three-page English resume with an empty photo field tells a Taiwanese employer immediately that this candidate does not understand the local culture.

**What to do instead:** the mainstream Taiwanese format is one to two pages, includes a headshot, is primarily in Chinese, and covers three core sections: education, work experience, and skills. See our guides on [resume writing for Taiwan](/blog/en/posts/resume-tips/) and [the complete Chinese resume guide](/blog/en/posts/chinese-resume-tips/).

## Mistake 3: ignoring the work permit process until an offer arrives

Many overseas students only discover after receiving an offer that work permit review takes time, or that they are not certain their degree meets the eligibility criteria. Start dates slip again and again, and sometimes the employer gives up waiting.

**What to do instead:** confirm your work permit eligibility before you start applying in earnest. See [the work permit application process](/blog/en/posts/work-permit-apply/) and [the complete work permit guide](/blog/en/posts/work-permit-guide/).

---

## Mistake 4: preparing only your self-introduction for interviews

Taiwanese interviews very often include "What do you know about our company?", "Where do you think your strengths lie?", and "What salary are you expecting?" Many candidates have not prepared for any of them.

**What to do instead:** before the interview, research the company's main business, recent news, and the scope of the role. Decide in advance how you will answer the salary question rather than saying "anything is fine" on the spot.

![A job seeker preparing for an interview](https://res.cloudinary.com/dyebbsckc/image/upload/f_auto,q_auto:good,w_1200,c_limit/wport-blog/taiwan-interview-tips-cover.jpg)

---

## Mistake 5: waiting for jobs to come to you instead of building a network

In Taiwan, a great many openings surface through personal referrals rather than job platforms alone. Many overseas students default to applying online and overlook how much knowing people matters.

**What to do instead:** actively attend industry events, alumni gatherings, or themed events like Charging Station. LinkedIn is an important channel too, but only if you genuinely engage rather than creating an account and leaving it idle.

---

## Summary

The advantages overseas students bring to the Taiwanese job market are clear: language ability, cross-cultural background, and an international outlook, all of which Taiwanese employers value more every year. The problem is usually not whether you can do the job. It is whether you have articulated those advantages clearly enough for the other side to see them.

Avoid these five mistakes and your job search will get noticeably more efficient.
