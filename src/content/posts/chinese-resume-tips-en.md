Chinese resumes in Taiwan differ from English resumes in a number of formatting conventions. If you simply translate your English resume into Chinese and send it off, HR will immediately see that you are unfamiliar with local hiring practice, and your first impression takes a hit.

This guide walks you through writing a Chinese resume that resonates with Taiwanese employers.

---

## Format: one to two pages, with a photo

The standard Taiwanese resume is one to two pages long and includes a headshot, usually against a white or light background. The photo is not optional. Many HR staff feel a resume is incomplete the moment they notice there is no photo.

Choose a photo taken within the last two years. Neat clothing and a natural expression are enough. It does not need to look like a formal ID photo.

---

## Required sections

A Taiwanese Chinese resume usually contains the following sections.

**1. Basic information**<br>Name, contact details (email and mobile), city of residence (the city or county is enough), and a LinkedIn or portfolio link.

**2. Education**<br>Listing your highest degree is enough. The format is "school name + major + graduation year (or expected graduation)". For an overseas degree, add a short note in parentheses, for example "XXX University, United States".

**3. Work experience**<br>Write each role as bullet points that make two things clear: what you did and what came out of it. Do not stop at "responsible for XX". Write "achieved XX result through XX approach".

**4. Skills**<br>Split these into language ability (Chinese and English levels, other languages) and professional skills (software, tools, certifications).

## The autobiography: do you need one?

Many Taiwanese application forms ask for an autobiography (自傳) of roughly 300 to 500 characters. This is not a university personal statement. It should cover who you are, what you have done, and why you want to work here.

A common structure:

- Opening: a brief introduction to your background
- Middle: one or two experiences relevant to this job
- Closing: what you hope to get from this role or company

It does not need to be long. Clarity and focus matter more than length.

---

## What overseas students should pay extra attention to

**Be specific about language ability**: do not just write "fluent Chinese". Write something like "Chinese (native level / fluent in daily communication)" and "English (business communication)" so HR can gauge your level at a glance.

**Work permit status**: if you already hold legal residency or work eligibility, add a line in the basic information section such as "holds valid work permit" or "eligible to work while enrolled". State it up front rather than leaving HR to guess.

**Degree accreditation**: if your overseas degree has been accredited by the Ministry of Education, note that next to the education entry. It removes a common source of hesitation for HR.

## Summary

The core of a Chinese resume is not translating your content into Chinese. It is presenting your value the way Taiwanese HR teams expect to read it. Get the format right and keep the content clear, and your resume is already ahead of most of the competition.
